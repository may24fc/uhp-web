Sleep

Better Sleep When Your Business Never Switches Off

You can be exhausted all day and still struggle to switch off at night. When the laptop closes but your brain keeps running through tomorrow's meetings, staff issues and unfinished work, better sleep isn't just about going to bed earlier. It's about creating an off-switch.

March 2026
7 min read
Sleep

It's 10:15PM. You've sent the last email. Then you remember something you need to do tomorrow. You pick up your phone. One message becomes three. You check tomorrow's calendar. Then you finally get into bed. But work has finished everywhere except inside your head. Tomorrow's meeting. That proposal. A staffing issue. School drop-off. Something you forgot to reply to. Something you absolutely cannot forget tomorrow. You're physically tired. Your brain isn't. And even when you spend eight hours in bed, you sometimes wake up wondering why you don't feel particularly refreshed. For business owners, sleep can become another casualty of an overloaded calendar. But improving it doesn't necessarily mean creating an elaborate 90-minute nighttime routine. Start by giving your brain and body a clearer message: The working day is over.

1. Start with tomorrow morning

When people decide to improve their sleep, they often start with: “What time should I go to bed?” But your wake-up time matters too. Australia's current 24-hour movement guidelines recommend 7–9 hours of good-quality sleep for adults aged 18–64, with consistent bed and wake-up times. Department of Health That doesn't mean you need to join the 5AM club. And it doesn't mean waking at exactly 6:32AM every day. It means giving your sleep some consistency. If you're waking at 6:30AM during the working week but sleeping until 9:30AM every Saturday and Sunday, your sleep timing is changing considerably. Instead, aim for a reasonably consistent schedule that still fits your life. You don't need the earliest wake-up time. You need one you can sustain.

2. Create a shutdown between business and bed

Here's a difficult transition: 9:58PM: analysing a business problem. 10:00PM: close laptop. 10:01PM: sleep. Your brain doesn't necessarily operate like your MacBook. Healthdirect recommends establishing a “buffer zone” before bedtime when you're no longer solving problems or planning the next day, and avoiding your computer or phone during that period. Health Direct Think of it as your shutdown period. It doesn't need to be complicated. You could:

  • Close the laptop
  • Write down anything important for tomorrow
  • Put your work phone away
  • Have a shower
  • Read
  • Talk with your partner
  • Do something quiet that doesn't require another decision

You're creating a transition: WORK → SHUTDOWN → SLEEP For someone running a business, that boundary matters. Because without one, work can expand until the moment you close your eyes.

3. Get tomorrow out of your head

This one's for the business owner who becomes incredibly productive the second their head touches the pillow. Suddenly: “I need to call that client.” Then: “Did I approve that invoice?” Followed by: “What are we doing about Thursday's meeting?” You don't need to solve tomorrow tonight. Instead, before your shutdown period, take five minutes and write down: TOMORROW'S TOP 3 1. Send proposal 2. Call supplier 3. Review presentation Add anything else you're worried about forgetting to your task list. Then close it. You're not trying to finish tomorrow's work. You're giving tomorrow's work somewhere to live other than your head.

4. Audit the 3PM coffee before blaming your sleep

Coffee isn't automatically the enemy. For many business owners, it's part of the working day. But timing matters. If you're using caffeine late in the afternoon to survive because you're tired—and then struggling to sleep that night—you may be creating a cycle. Healthdirect recommends avoiding tea, coffee and caffeinated soft drinks for at least 3–7 hours before bed. It also notes that caffeine affects people differently. Health Direct So don't immediately declare: “No more coffee.” Run an experiment. If your final coffee is normally at 4PM, move it earlier for a week. Then see what happens. And remember that caffeine isn't just coffee. It can also be found in:

  • Tea
  • Energy drinks
  • Cola
  • Chocolate
  • Some workout products

The goal isn't eliminating everything you enjoy. It's finding out what's actually affecting you.

5. Be careful when alcohol becomes your “off-switch”

You've finished a long day. You pour a drink. You finally start feeling relaxed. Understandable. But there's a difference between feeling sleepy and getting good-quality sleep. Healthdirect notes that alcohol may initially make you sleepy but can disrupt sleep cycles and reduce sleep quality. Its sleep guidance recommends avoiding alcohol for at least four hours before bed. Health Direct That doesn't mean you can't enjoy a drink at dinner or a client event. But if: “I need a drink to switch off” has quietly become part of your nightly routine, it's worth noticing. Experiment with another off-switch. Walk after dinner. Take a shower. Read. Talk to your partner. Or simply close the laptop and don't reopen it.

6. Make your bedroom bad for business

Your bedroom doesn't need: A sleep tracker on every surface. A $500 pillow. Blackout technology designed by NASA. It needs to make sleeping easier. Healthdirect recommends keeping the bedroom comfortable, quiet and dark, and keeping known stressors—such as work tools and laptops—out of the room where possible. Health Direct That's particularly relevant for business owners. Because if you're lying in bed: Answering emails. Checking tomorrow's calendar. Reviewing numbers. Scrolling LinkedIn. Watching videos. You're giving your brain plenty to do. Try making the bedroom bad for business. No laptop. No proposal. No “quick email.” Tomorrow's work can wait until tomorrow.

What if you wake up at 3AM?

You wake up. You look at the clock. 3:07AM. Then the calculation starts. “If I fall asleep right now, I've still got three hours and 23 minutes.” 3:19AM. “Great. Now I've only got three hours and 11 minutes.” You're no longer sleeping. You're doing sleep accounting. Healthdirect recommends avoiding clock-watching because it can increase anxiety about not sleeping. Its insomnia guidance also recommends getting up and doing a quiet activity if you can't sleep, then returning to bed when you're tired. Health Direct Keep things quiet. Keep the lights low. Read something. Then return to bed when you feel sleepy. Most importantly: Don't decide 3:15AM is an excellent opportunity to get ahead on emails.

What if your week is genuinely chaotic?

This is where generic sleep advice often falls apart. Wednesday: client dinner. Thursday: early flight. Friday: one of the kids wakes during the night. Saturday: social event. You're not going to execute exactly the same evening routine 365 nights a year. That's okay. Focus on the things you can control most often: WAKE TIME Keep it reasonably consistent. SHUTDOWN Create some separation between work and sleep. CAFFEINE Know roughly when you're stopping. BEDROOM Keep work out where possible. OPPORTUNITY Give yourself enough time to obtain the sleep you need. RESET After a disrupted night, return to your normal routine rather than abandoning it. The aim isn't perfection. It's having a default routine to return to.

Try this tonight: the 30-minute shutdown

Don't overhaul your sleep. Run one experiment tonight. 1. Choose your shutdown time Pick a realistic time when business finishes for the night. 2. Write tomorrow's top three Get the important things out of your head. 3. Close the business Laptop away. Client messages finished. No more decisions unless something is genuinely urgent. 4. Spend the next 30 minutes winding down Read. Shower. Talk to your partner. Stretch. Do something quiet. Then go to bed. Do it consistently enough to see whether it makes a difference.

When it's worth getting help

Lifestyle habits aren't the explanation for every sleep problem. If you regularly wake feeling unrefreshed, frequently struggle to fall asleep, feel restless in bed or tiredness is affecting your mood or daily life, speak with your GP. Healthdirect specifically recommends seeking medical advice in these circumstances. Health Direct Persistent sleep problems can have causes that aren't going to be solved by simply putting your phone away earlier.

The UHP Takeaway

For a business owner, sleep isn't wasted time. It's recovery time. And you don't need a perfect morning routine, an elaborate nighttime ritual or a life with zero stress to start improving it. You need enough consistency around the things you can control. Protect your sleep opportunity. Create a shutdown from work. Pay attention to caffeine and alcohol. Keep business out of the bedroom. And when life disrupts the routine—as it inevitably will—return to it. At The Ultimate Health Project, we look at sleep as part of the bigger health and performance system. Because your business will still have another email tomorrow. Another decision. Another meeting. Another problem that needs solving. Your recovery deserves a place in the calendar too.
